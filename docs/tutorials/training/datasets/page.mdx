# Datasets

A dataset is a pile of episodes. An episode is one take of the task: the wrist video, the arm and gripper readings, and the actions that were happening, from the moment you start until you say the take is over. {{ className: 'lead' }}

On disk, a session the robot recorded looks like this. The full layout is [Recording](/docs/recording).

```text
recordings/robot/0004_2b8e51_pick/
├── session.json
├── manifest.jsonl
└── episodes/000000/
    ├── episode.json
    ├── episode.parquet
    └── video/right_wrist_camera.rgb_image.mp4
```

`episode.parquet` is the numbers: joint positions, gripper width, actions, timestamps. The mp4 is the camera at the resolution the sensor produced, not the small crop the policy happens to want today. You can train a different crop later. You cannot invent pixels a downscale threw away.

A Lumi recorder writes a different file, on purpose. It is an MP4 with the motion in a side track, the shape UMI's pipeline already accepts. [Collect](/docs/tutorials/training/collect) turns that into the session above. You do not hand-convert frames.

## What a good take looks like

The policy imitates what you recorded. It will also imitate the bad habits.

- **One task per dataset.** "Pick up the red block" is a dataset. "Pick up the red block, and also stack cups" is two datasets that will teach a confused average.
- **The object stays in the wrist camera.** If you can't see it, the policy can't. Start the take with the object in frame, not after a blind reach.
- **Vary the start a little.** Same spot every time teaches a policy that only works on that spot. Shift the object a few centimetres between takes.
- **Finish the task.** A take that stops halfway is a demonstration of stopping halfway. Drop those. The last chapter has the command.
- **Keep the misses that you recovered from.** A take where the fingers bumped the object and then grasped it is useful. A take where you gave up is not.

Fifty short, finished episodes beat five long, perfect ones. You will feel this when the first example run finishes and the second, on your own data, does not.
