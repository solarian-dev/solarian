# Going further

You now have the vocabulary of the course. This last chapter points at the topics worth reading next, and at the Solarian ideas the later tutorials introduce. {{ className: 'lead' }}

## Topics to read next

These topics sit underneath the earlier chapters. Treat them as optional reading: each one is a good next step when its name shows up.

**The Jacobian.** A matrix that says how fast the hand moves when each joint moves a little. One column per joint: column *i* is the hand's velocity when only joint *i* turns. The inverse kinematics figure in the [kinematics chapter](/docs/tutorials/gentle-intro/kinematics) runs on it: every step of its search turns the joints along the Jacobian toward the target.

**Singularities.** Configurations where the arm loses a direction of motion. Stretch the arm out straight, as it is along the white edge of the workspace figure in the same chapter, and its joints can only swing the hand around that edge; pulling it straight back toward the base is impossible. Solvers asked for that direction demand huge joint speeds, so an arm near a singularity twitches or stalls.

**Three ways to solve inverse kinematics.**

- *Analytical:* solve the geometry on paper, as with the law of cosines on the two-link arm. Exact and fast, and specific to one arm's layout.
- *Numerical:* guess the joints, run forward kinematics, and adjust until the hand is close enough. Works for any arm.
- *Differential:* use the Jacobian to take a small joint step toward the target, and repeat. This is what `MinkIK` does.

**Gravity compensation.** Computing, from the link masses, the torque that holds the arm up, and adding it to every command so the feedback only has to fix what the model missed. Torque-controlled arms do this. The SO-106's servos are position-controlled, and the I term in their PID does the holding instead.

**Motion planning.** Searching for a path that reaches the goal without hitting anything. *Sampling-based* planners draw random configurations and connect the collision-free ones. *Optimization-based* planners start from a rough path and push it away from obstacles. Both get harder as the scene fills up and the arm gains joints.

## What is still ahead

The course introduces a few more Solarian ideas as it needs them:

- **Plugins** are packages that add a module, a skill, or a motor driver. Tutorial 2 installs the Feetech driver and the gripper. See [Installing a plugin](/docs/plugins#installing-a-plugin).
- A **skill** is a behavior with a start and a finish: go to a pose, run a policy. [Tutorial 4](/docs/tutorials/skills).
- A **service** is a long-running helper a skill calls, such as the process that holds a trained policy in memory.
- A **dataset** is a collection of episodes, each one a recording of observations and actions. Recording writes episodes. Training fits a policy to them. [Tutorial 5](/docs/tutorials/training).
